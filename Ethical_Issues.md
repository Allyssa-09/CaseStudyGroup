### **Ethical Issues**

#### **Introduction**
As social media shifts from Instagram photos to TikTok videos, the ethical issues around these platforms have grown. As social media got bigger, companies used more AI and more data to keep people hooked. But that also created problems for users' wellbeing. This section looks at how these changes increased ethical problems and suggest practical ways to make social media more responsible.

#### **Privacy Concerns**
While Instagram used data mainly for advertising, but TikTok's situation is more serious since it operates under Chinese law. China's 2017 National Intelligence Law can force companies to support the government, meaning user data could be at risk of being accessed by a foreign government (Congressional Research Service, 2020).

In 2022, ByteDance employees wrongly accessed TikTok data to track the locations of several journalists, confirming many privacy concerns (Price,2018). After this, the US, UK, EU, and Canada banned TikTok on government devices (Reuters, 2023). Investigations also showed that TikTok can collect large amounts of user data, including biometric details and location information (Forbes,2021).

#### **Mental Health Impact**
Instagram pushed people to aim for perfect posts. TikTok made things more intense with an algorithm built to keep users hooked. Studies show that endless scrolling and personalised videos can trigger dopamine and lead to addictive use (BBC Future, 2023).

Leaked research shows that Instagram makes body image worse for 1 in 3 teen girls (The Verge,2021). The Centre for Countering Digital Hate also found that TikTok's algorithm can start showing harmful content to teens within minutes, including eating disorder videos that have billions of views (CCDH,2022). Studies also show that social media addiction can look like substance addiction (Psychology Today, 2022).

#### **Content Safety **
Instagram struggled with manually checking posts, but TikTok's algorithm caused even bigger moderation problems. The platform automatically promotes content that gets the most engagement, not necessarily what is accurate or safe, which can quickly spread harmful material (The Guardian, 2023).

TikTok's algorithm has been known to push risky challenges and harmful content to teens (The Guardian, 2023). During COVID-19, the app also became a centre for medical misinformation, with a lot of popular videos spreading false claims (The Journal of Medical Internet Research, 2021). Basically, the way the algorithm works can quickly take users from normal content to more extreme or dangerous stuff (Centre for Countering Digital Hate, 2022).

#### **Solutions**
**For Data Privacy**
TikTok should have outside audits of its recommendation algorithm, with the results shared publicly, like the rules in the EU Digital Services Act (European Parliament, 2022).

**For Mental Health**
Make users take breaks after 45 minutes of continuous use and offer a chronological feed to reduce algorithm-driven pressure. Platforms should also make well-being tools and usage stats easy to access and understand (American Psychological Association, 2023).

**For Content Moderation**
Platforms should release quarterly reports showing what their algorithms are boosting, not just what they remove. They should also have independent oversight boards with real authority over content decisions (Tech Policy Press, 2022).

#### **Conclusion**
In conclusion, the ethical issues that grew from Instagram to TikTok are built into how these platforms make money. Solving these issues requires moving away from engagement at all costs and toward giving users more control, clear information, and safer experiences.
